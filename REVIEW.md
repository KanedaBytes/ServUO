**1. Findings that should change the next work briefs**

**F1 — High priority, current defect: the behavior ticker starts twice.** ServUO discovers and calls every public static `Initialize` method. `BotSystem.Initialize` also explicitly calls `BotTickManager.Initialize`, whose implementation unconditionally creates another repeating timer and replaces its stored handle. Both callbacks remain scheduled. The default is therefore approximately two behavior passes every two seconds, not the documented single pass. Initialization order does not rescue this: either order creates two timers. [ScriptCompiler.cs:87](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Server/ScriptCompiler.cs:87), [BotSystem.cs:99](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Bots/BotSystem.cs:99), [BotTickManager.cs:54](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Bots/BotTickManager.cs:54), [BotTickManager.cs:69](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Bots/BotTickManager.cs:69).

The planning allowance is reset on each pass, and behaviors are invoked from that pass. Consequently, tick-based attempts and allowances differ from the intended cadence; elapsed-time deadlines need not run twice as fast. **Recommendation/inference:** remove the extra bootstrap call or rename the explicitly called helper, and make startup idempotent. Cost: hours plus a runtime cadence check. Risk: existing population and probe baselines incorporated the extra passes, so recalibrate them rather than restoring the bug to recover old numbers. [BotTickManager.cs:120](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Bots/BotTickManager.cs:120), [BotTickManager.cs:180](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Bots/BotTickManager.cs:180).

**F2 — High priority before PK/combat, conditional current defect: a bot can enter a player-death handler that casts it to PlayerMobile.** `PlayerBot` inherits `BaseCreature` but sets `Player = true`. `Mobile.OnDeath` raises `PlayerDeath` on the player branch. `ReportMurderer` handles that event and casts the victim to `PlayerMobile` when there is a reportable player aggressor and the SE rules apply. A bot victim meeting those conditions cannot satisfy that cast. This is a concrete type incompatibility, not a claim that every bot death currently crashes. The bot calls `base.OnDeath` before releasing its mount or scheduling its deletion, so an exception on that path can also interrupt its own cleanup. **Recommendation/inference:** make reportable death a mandatory test in the identity spike. Until the class decision is implemented, do not advertise PK parity or enable that feature on the strength of a basic kill probe. Cost: a small targeted reproduction; the permanent repair belongs to the identity work described below. Risk: simply removing `Player = true` would change other behavior, including the party gate. **F3 — High priority for admin reliability, current defect: requests can overwrite or delete one another.** The bridge publishes directly to a fixed token filename for an operation. The shard reads that filename, executes the request, then deletes it. If request B replaces the file after A was read but before A finishes, A deletes B. Two requests published before the next poll also collapse into one. Nonces stop a caller accepting some other caller's acknowledgement; they do not preserve requests. Direct writes additionally expose a truncate/write window to the other process. [bridge.js:902](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:902), [bridge.js:919](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:919), [bridge.js:941](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:941), [RequestPoller.cs:116](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/Bridge/RequestPoller.cs:116), [RequestPoller.cs:131](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/Bridge/RequestPoller.cs:131).

**Recommendation/inference:** keep the file transport but give each request its own ID, atomically publish it, claim it before dispatch, and acknowledge that ID. Include operation, payload, expiry, and where relevant the expected configuration hash or boot identity. Define replay handling for destructive operations. A per-operation busy response and atomic publication are useful interim protections but do not solve every crash or multi-writer case. Cost: several days across both implementations and tests. **2. Architecture and the editor boundary**

Inference: Core's reusable services, a navigation owner, domain behaviors, and a separate editor process are sensible boundaries. `LoopQueue` centralizes game-thread dispatch; navigation owns loaded graph state and preserves the previous graph on load failure; the editor uses named whitelist operations instead of accepting arbitrary paths. These choices reduce the number of places that need to understand world-thread and filesystem rules. Retain them. [LoopQueue.cs:150](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/LoopQueue.cs:150), [NavigationSystem.cs:10](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/Navigation/NavigationSystem.cs:10), [bridge.js:1193](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:1193).

The most fragile dependency is below the domain layer: `NavWalker` owns a `BaseCreature`, takes one in its constructor, and moves through `AIObject`, `NextMove`, and `MoveTo`. That makes a supposedly reusable navigation service depend on one actor inheritance tree and its scheduler. `BotShove` also combines movement permission and diagnostic classification. **Recommendation/inference:** extract the smallest locomotion adapter needed by a PlayerMobile spike: actor position/map, attempt a step, movement deadline, door policy, and cleanup. Keep routing and behavior contracts intact. Cost: days for the adapter, weeks for migration and parity verification; risk is changing movement timing or collision semantics. [NavWalker.cs:128](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/Navigation/NavWalker.cs:128), [NavWalker.cs:216](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/Navigation/NavWalker.cs:216), [NavWalker.cs:643](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/Navigation/NavWalker.cs:643), [BotShove.cs:95](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Bots/BotShove.cs:95).

The queue's timeout does not cancel queued work: its callback still calls `work()` if the game loop later drains it. The token bridge has the analogous ambiguity around dispatch, deletion, and acknowledgement. **Inference:** callers must distinguish “failed before execution,” “completed,” and “outcome unknown.” Retrying a timed-out purchase, save, or shutdown as though nothing happened is unsafe. This is a general contract to establish before reusable economy actions, rather than a reason to add locks around game-thread code. [LoopQueue.cs:177](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/LoopQueue.cs:177), [LoopQueue.cs:197](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/LoopQueue.cs:197), [RequestPoller.cs:117](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/Scripts/Custom/Core/Bridge/RequestPoller.cs:117).

Inference: the file bridge is a reasonable long-term choice for a local, low-throughput editor. Its one-second polling and disk snapshots fit administrative operations; replacing it with HTTP inside the shard would introduce another thread boundary without automatically fixing delivery semantics. Treat it as a small command protocol, not a collection of magic filenames. A remote, multi-user control plane would need a separate authenticated boundary and an explicit deployment design. **Security finding:** loopback binding is real, but “same-origin” protection is overstated. With `Sec-Fetch-Site` present the bridge accepts `same-site` and `none` as well as `same-origin`, and does not consult `Origin`. Different ports can be same-site while being different origins. The request dispatcher does not enforce an allowed `Host`. These are relevant because this process can request shutdown and invoke the restart launcher. This review did not demonstrate a browser exploit. [bridge.js:264](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:264), [bridge.js:677](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:677), [bridge.js:1036](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:1036), [bridge.js:1105](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:1105).

**Recommendation/inference:** keep the loopback bind; validate `Host` and exact allowed origins, and use a per-session secret for privileged requests. Decide explicitly how authenticated command-line calls work. Test cross-port same-site requests, conflicting metadata, missing metadata, and unexpected hostnames. Host validation also reduces DNS-rebinding exposure, whose practical exploitability depends on browser and network behavior. Cost: a day or two, with risk of breaking an undocumented curl workflow. Do not equate CORS response blocking with preventing a side effect. [bridge.js:267](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:267), [bridge.js:273](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.js:273), [bridge.test.js:421](E:/dev/UO/ServUO-shard/tools/editor/bridge.test.js:421).

Its process detection is based on the `ServUO` process name, and the PowerShell launcher starts a build window rather than waiting for a successful build itself. The bridge's later health handshake is consequently important. **Inference:** before multiple installations or Linux hosting, identify the intended process and new boot explicitly and put launching behind a platform adapter. Cost: days; no justification for changing hosting now. Custom persistence failures are caught rather than propagated. **Questions the owner should settle**

- **Inference — identity:** when a named bot ends a session, is that person offline, retired, or destroyed? Should another bot inherit their property? This determines whether session churn may replace Mobile objects and whether account ownership is individual or communal.
- **Inference — fairness:** which player rules should bots obey exactly: skill anti-macro, reagent costs, murder reports, account house limits, item loss, and vendor wages? “Behave like players” does not resolve those choices.
- **Inference — economy:** what are the permitted sources and sinks of currency and resources, and what total must remain conserved across a purchase, interrupted delivery, restart, and retirement? Who owns assets when an operation's outcome is unknown?
- **Inference — recovery:** may a bot carrying purchased goods or fighting a player ever receive an administrative recovery teleport? If so, what prevents that from becoming a gameplay advantage?
- **Inference — operating target:** what population, active-walker percentage, connected-player count, and acceptable worst-case pause define success? Without these, neither a pathfinder replacement nor a scale test has a stopping condition.
- **Inference — deployment and distribution:** will the editor remain local to a trusted operator, and will binaries/source be shared outside that environment? These answers determine the next security and licensing work; Linux hosting alone answers neither.
- **Inference — release evidence:** what exact combination of unit tests, engine probes, save/reload checks, and observed soak duration authorizes the next phase? Assign that gate before the phase begins, so a convenient green probe cannot silently become the definition of success.
